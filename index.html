HiveBoard: An Open, Modular, 3D-Printed Dexterity Benchmark for Industrial Robotic Manipulation

..., ..., ..., Ricardo V. Godoy, Marcelo Becker
Robotics Laboratory (LabRoM), São Carlos School of Engineering, University of São Paulo, Brazil
Corresponding author: ricardo.godoy@alumni.usp.br
PDF arXiv Files GitHub

Abstract

Manipulation benchmarks predominantly evaluate free-object grasping, pick-and-place, and loose-part assembly, yet real-world usefulness arises from constrained, force-dependent, often multi-step mechanisms. Performance on such tasks is poorly predicted by grasping scores, and existing physical benchmarks that cover mechanisms rely on purchased components, hindering reproducibility and modification. We present HiveBoard, an open, modular, fully 3D-printed benchmark that enables any laboratory to evaluate grippers, hands, and manipulation policies on functional mechanisms. A hexagonal honeycomb base accepts interchangeable attachments modeled on industrial fittings and organized into three skill categories: torque-based tasks (a ball valve with a four-piece friction ring set, two gate valves at different torque levels, and a circuit breaker), precision-based tasks (a light-bulb socket, two threaded fasteners of different sizes, and a peg-insertion plate), and composed assembly tasks (a push button hidden by a hinged cover, a key-and-lock mechanism, a sliding drawer, and a two-cell shock-absorber model). All 13 attachments are fully functional, printable on consumer-grade FDM printers, and released with articulated CAD models in URDF and USD for direct import into common simulators. We describe the design, manufacturing, and evaluation protocol, and report a cross-laboratory validation across five manipulation systems—quadruped- and fixed-base teleoperated arms, a legged manipulator driven via virtual-reality devices, and a wearable anthropomorphic prosthetic hand—scored on the same thirteen tasks. Results show that HiveBoard produces a broad difficulty gradient and that different embodiments fail on different attachments for interpretable, hardware-specific reasons.

Torque Tasks

Modules like the industrial valves evaluate the robot's grasping strength and continuous rotational dexterity.

Precision Insertion

Peg-in-hole and USB insertion tasks test fine motor control, alignment, and contact-rich manipulation capabilities.

Linear Resistance

Drawers and sliders measure the platform's ability to maintain steady velocity and force under varied friction.
